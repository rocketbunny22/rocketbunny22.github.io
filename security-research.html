Security Research

Threat Detection & Research

Connecting hands-on security research with endpoint monitoring, email security, and incident response.

By Derek Nolt

Connecting attacks to evidence

My interest in security research is rooted in a practical question: what does suspicious activity look like from the defender’s side? Understanding an attack technique is useful, but connecting it to logs, alerts, and the affected systems is what makes that knowledge useful during an investigation.

Tools in context

My work includes setting up Wazuh and Microsoft 365 Defender for endpoint and email monitoring. These tools provide different views of an environment. An alert is a starting point for understanding what happened, which systems were involved, and whether the activity fits normal behavior.

Learning through lab work

Hack The Box Academy and home lab work support my ongoing study of bug bounty methodology, Active Directory, and network environments. These areas connect to the systems I work with in IT operations and give me room to explore how configuration choices affect security.

Keeping the focus on response

Helping restore operations after a ransomware incident reinforced the connection between monitoring and hands-on troubleshooting. My research interests follow that same direction: understand the path an attacker could take, recognize useful evidence, and connect the findings to changes that help protect the environment.
